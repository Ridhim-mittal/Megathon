# Village storyline — story bible

**Mallepalli**, a cotton village in Telangana. One kirana shop, one bus a day, one big tank (cheruvu)
that floods some years and dries up in others. Your family rents two acres, has no savings, owes
the kirana shop, and has never been inside a bank. "No money, no knowledge." Poverty is never your
fault. The story is about learning to see clearly.

## The wound

The family story: "We lost Thatha's land the year the rains failed." Everyone repeats it. Nanna
won't talk about it. Ammamma goes quiet when the sahukar walks past.

## The twist (revealed at 32–34, by the tin box)

It wasn't the rains. Twenty years ago Ammamma fell ill. There was no insurance and no emergency
money. Thatha borrowed ₹15,000 from a town lender at "only ₹3 per ₹100 a month" and put his
thumbprint on papers no one in the house could read. The interest was compound (chakravaddi). In
four years ₹15,000 became over ₹60,000, and the papers turned out to be a sale deed, not a loan
paper. The land went. Ammamma has always believed it was her fault.

She learnt to read so she would never sign blind again. Her glasses were the first thing she bought.
In the tin box she kept the old papers. When the player shops for the dream at 33, the plot on offer
is **Survey No. 212 — Thatha's land**, and Lucky Prakash is the broker. Reading the documents with the
glasses now protects the family from the same trap, a generation later.

## Arc by stage

- **Pocket money (11–14).** Chocolates, game top-ups, the tin box vs a bank account, the price of
  a chocolate going up, Lucky Prakash's "free game coins" link. Ammamma's chakravaddi lesson with
  seeds gives the first hint of the wound.
- **First choices (15–18).** Chintu's phone on EMI, cotton-picking vs tuition, a UPI scam when
  selling your old cycle, a cricket betting app. Crossroads 1 at 17–18: degree with an education
  loan, the spinning mill now, an ITI electrician course, or Chintu's shortcut with Lucky Prakash.
- **First income (19–24).** First payslip, PF and PAN. An emergency fund. A day at the sahukar's desk
  (Sahukar's chair). Lucky Prakash's "rent your bank account" offer. Ammamma's hospital scare and
  insurance. Starting a small SIP. Teach-backs: Chinni asks your advice.
- **Storms (25–30).** A cousin's grand wedding and the pull to match it. Crossroads 2: move to
  Hyderabad, take a Gulf job through an agent, stay and grow the farm, or start a small business. A
  market crash, a flood or a hospital bill, Policy Ramana's "investment plus insurance" plan, Lucky
  Prakash's "double your money" club.
- **The dream (31–36).** The big purchase. Crossroads 3: how to pay and which papers to trust, with
  the twist. Pension for Amma and Nanna, nominees and KYC, and Chinni's own future.

## Chintu's rival life

Phone on EMI at 16. Betting app at 17. Drops out at 18 and follows Lucky Prakash. Loan-app trap at 21.
Nearly jailed as a money mule at 22, then calls 1930 and comes clean. Delivery rider, then a mobile
repair stall on a Mudra loan. At 36 his dream is six years late, but he's laughing again. He is the
proof that every bad path has a way back.

## Endings

The engine picks the ending. Content gives each ending its feeling:
- `dream_on_time`: the family photo in front of the dream, Bujji photobombing.
- `dream_with_debt`: the dream is real, but so are the EMIs on the wall calendar.
- `dream_close`: almost there, and safe. Ammamma: "A tree doesn't fruit the year you plant it."
- `secure_no_dream`: no dream yet, but no fear either. Insurance, savings, no sahukar.
- `debt_trap`: the khata notebook comes back. A way out exists, and the last panel shows the first step.
- `wrong_path`: the way back — 1930, coming clean, starting again.

## Storyteller reveal

The narrator was **Chinni** all along, now a nurse, telling your story to the Saturday money club at
Mallepalli school. She is wearing Ammamma's glasses. Her last line: "My big one never had money.
They had questions. That was enough."

## Tone

Warm, funny, short sentences, real numbers. Villains are schemes, not communities. Every heavy moment
has a helper nearby: family, Kavitha akka at the bank, or the 1930 cyber-crime helpline.
